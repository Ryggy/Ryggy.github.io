Dino Sim - Procedural Ecosystem Simulation
Role: Programmer
Team Size: 3 | Keywords: Unity (C#), A* Pathfinding, GOAP, Simulation
Overview
Dino Sim is a Unity-based simulation that procedurally generates a living, self-sustaining ecosystem populated by AI-driven dinosaurs. Each dinosaur operates using a Goal-Oriented Action Planning (GOAP) system and a custom A* pathfinding implementation, enabling autonomous decision-making and realistic navigation across dynamic terrain.
Dinosaurs make decisions to satisfy needs such as hunger, thirst, and energy - producing emergent predator-prey dynamics and believable survival behaviour. Players can explore the simulation through an interactive Dino Viewer, following individual dinosaurs and monitoring their stats and actions.

My Contributions
  • Developed a complete procedural world generation pipeline, including:
    • Terrain Generation: Used Perlin noise to generate varied landscapes with configurable water levels.
    • Tree Placement: Implemented a fitness function considering height, slope, proximity to water, and random noise for natural distribution.
    • Dinosaur Placement: Randomly spawned dinosaurs on valid terrain positions.
  • Designed and programmed a hybrid GOAP–State Manager AI system:
    • State Manager controlled high-level needs (hunger, thirst, fatigue).
    • GOAP system handled low-level task planning (finding food, navigating to it, eating, resting, drinking).
  • Created custom A* pathfinding to enable dynamic navigation without relying on Unity’s NavMesh or third-party plugins.
  • Implemented species-specific behaviour:
    • Stegosaurus herbivores feed on trees.
    • Raptors hunt Stegosaurus and rest near vegetation.
    • Both species require water and energy management to survive.
  • Built an interactive Dino Viewer allowing users to focus on specific dinosaurs, view live stats, and observe AI decision cycles.
Tools & Technologies
Unity (C#), GOAP AI, A* Pathfinding, Procedural Generation, Perlin Noise
Focus
This project explored how procedural generation, goal-oriented AI, and custom pathfinding algorithms can combine to simulate emergent, believable ecosystems. It deepened my understanding of AI architecture, systemic design, and optimised navigation without relying on built-in Unity systems.
